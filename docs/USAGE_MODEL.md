# Water usage model and anomaly detection

Stage 7, offline part: a model of normal water use learned from the flow history on hc-data, and an anomaly
detector calibrated on it. Nothing here touches the device or its protection tiers; the thresholds are an
input for a later Tier 2 / server-side alert step. Generated by `server/model/report.py` from the outputs of
`train.py` and `anomaly.py` (2026-09-30); how to run it: `server/model/README.md`.

## Answers first

- **Thursday 17:00**: expected **13 L** in the hour (median 7 L, 90 % of such hours below 28 L), 3.3 flows.
- **A 15 min flow at 03:00** is an anomaly: at night one flow alarms after **3 min** or 20 L. On the test year every injected 6 L/min x 15 min flow at Tuesday 03:00 was caught, after 3.0 min.
- **False alarms**: 1.19 per month on the test period (0.78 on validation, where the thresholds were set).

## Data

- 271,026 flows from 2020-06-19 to 2026-09-30: 269,551 from the legacy firmware (via BigQuery), 1,475 from firmware 3.x.
- One row per hour (55,056 hours, Europe/Warsaw calendar on a UTC grid, so DST neither drops nor doubles an hour). A flow crossing an hour boundary is split in proportion to time.
- Masked, never counted as zero use:
  - **telemetry gaps** longer than 24 h without a single flow (7,372 hours, mostly 2022);
  - **known unusual events** (2,539 hours, `server/model/known_events.csv`): the 2023 lawn watering while it was established, and the long flows that look like pool filling or the burst garden hose.
- **Legacy counter wrap** (docs/INVENTORY.md #6): each wrap added ~80 L. The import fixed only flows above the meter maximum (49.5 L/min); firmware 3.x never averages more than ~26 L/min (peak `max_lpm` 26.4), so any legacy flow averaging more loses the fewest wraps that bring it under (443 flows, 37 m³). Durations and counts are not affected; volumes of long legacy flows remain the least certain part of the data.
- Flows under 0.1 L are left out of counts and per-flow models: firmware 3.2.1+ does not publish them, while 27 % of legacy flows are that small.

### Checked against the utility meter

The utility's main meter readings (42 consecutive billing intervals, 2023-03 to 2026-09, 1 m³ resolution) are the ground truth for volume. Only ratios are shown here; the readings themselves stay private (`server/model/meter.py`).

| Telemetry / meter | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw pulses | 1.36 | 1.63 | 1.73 | 1.75 |
| import correction only | 0.98 | 0.93 | 1.03 | 1.07 |
| full wrap fix (used) | 0.92 | 0.86 | 0.96 | 1.00 |

- **The legacy counter-wrap bug began in July 2023** (first `suspect` flow 2023-07-03): before it, raw and corrected pulses are the same (0.94 of the meter, the telemetry's usual loss); after it, raw pulses run at 1.4-1.7x the meter. Legacy volumes up to mid-2023 are not affected by the bug.
- **Legacy telemetry lost water**: with the full wrap fix it counted the pre-bug share again in 2023 and a clear deficit in 2024. The import-only correction lands near 1.00 overall only because residual wraps offset the lost flows.
- **The calibration holds**: the 2026 intervals imply 410 pulses per liter against the 410 in use.
- **Use is flat**: the meter shows no growth since mid-2023, so the apparent growth of about 10 % a year in the telemetry was lost data, not the household.
- **Reconciliation**: legacy flow volumes in each billing interval (until 2026-09-15) are scaled by meter / telemetry over that interval and its two neighbours (factor 0.97-1.23, mean 1.08); durations and counts are untouched, firmware 3.x is used as measured. Per interval the ratio scatters by 8 %, about what 1 m³ in 12 m³ allows.
- **Ongoing calibration check**: once firmware 3.x has 36 m³ of complete meter intervals (about ±3 %), `meter.py` recommends a new pulses-per-liter value if the implied one differs by more than 3 % (not enough firmware 3.x intervals yet).

## Hourly usage model

Features are the calendar only, so a forecast exists for any future hour: hour, weekday, Polish public holiday, bridge day, and optionally the season (day of year as sin/cos). Candidates:

- **B0** mean per hour of week, what the `usage_hour_of_week` view already gives (baseline);
- **B1** B0 with holidays treated as Sundays;
- **M1** Poisson GLM on hour-of-week dummies;
- **M2** gradient boosting (Poisson loss) on the calendar features.

Two hyperparameters on top:
- **recency weights**: the last 2 years count fully, each further year back by a decay (0, 0.25, 0.5 or 0.75), nothing beyond 5 years; "all data" is the unweighted reference. The household changed (the kids grew up), so older years describe it less well;
- a **level correction** that scales the forecast by actual / predicted over the preceding 28 or 56 days.

Best of each model on validation (liters per hour):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| M2 GBM (15 leaves, 300 it) | decay 0.25 | off | 17.574 | 12.63 |
| M2 GBM (15 leaves, 600 it) | decay 0.25 | off | 17.574 | 12.63 |
| M2 GBM (31 leaves, 300 it) | decay 0.25 | off | 17.599 | 12.64 |
| M2 GBM + season (15 leaves, 300 it) | decay 0.5 | 56 | 17.658 | 12.76 |
| M2 GBM + season (15 leaves, 600 it) | decay 0.5 | 56 | 17.658 | 12.76 |
| M1 Poisson GLM | last 2 y | off | 17.695 | 12.75 |

Chosen: **M2 GBM (15 leaves, 300 it)**, weights decay 0.25, no level correction, no season. For flows per hour: M2 GBM (15 leaves, 300 it), weights decay 0.5, no level correction. Every weighted variant beats the unweighted one on validation.

Test (2026), refitted on train + validation:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B0 hour-of-week mean | 21.76 | 13.70 | 28.0 | 123 | 14.2 / 17.7 |
| chosen model | 20.37 | 13.82 | 27.5 | 109 | 16.2 / 17.7 |
| chosen model + lags (comparison) | 19.79 | 13.77 | 27.2 | 107 | 16.5 / 17.7 |

| Flows per hour | Poisson deviance | MAE | RMSE | Daily MAE | Mean predicted / actual |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B0 hour-of-week mean | 3.65 | 2.97 | 4.4 | 21 | 4.1 / 4.3 |
| chosen model | 3.46 | 2.87 | 4.3 | 21 | 4.1 / 4.3 |

What that means:

- Deviance is 6 % lower than the baseline and the daily error drops from 123 to 109 L. With volumes reconciled to the meter, validation no longer picks the level correction: there is no trend left for it to follow.
- Hour-level MAE barely moves (it rewards the median, and the median hour is quiet). One hour of a household is mostly noise around a stable weekly shape; the model knows the shape, not whether someone showers at 17:00 or 17:40.
- The season does not help (every candidate got worse with it), as expected once the lawn is excluded.
- Lags (same hour a week ago, last 24 h) help a little more, but they only exist for the next hour, not for "Thursday 17:00".
- Prediction intervals hold on test: 87.7 % of hours fall under p90 and 98.2 % under p99.

## Anomaly detection

Each indicator is a one-sided threshold that depends on the local hour. For the upper ones it is simple: **the longest (biggest, fastest) normal flow ever seen at this hour or the neighbouring ones, times one shared margin**, with floors of 3 min and 20 L for one flow. The slow-flow indicator uses a low quantile (gradient boosting on hour, weekday, holiday). Every alarm stays explainable ("a 14 min flow where 12 min is the longest normal one at this hour") and the thresholds are small enough to live on the device.

| Indicator | Catches | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| duration | flow longer than usual: tap or hose left open, a leak at night | largest normal value at this hour ±1 h x 1.35 |
| volume | one flow bigger than usual | largest normal value at this hour ±1 h x 1.35 |
| rate_high | flow faster than usual (burst) | largest normal value at this hour ±1 h x 1.35 |
| hour_liters | hour total above usual: a hose with pauses | largest normal value at this hour ±1 h x 1.35 |
| rate_low | slow long flow (flows >= 5 min): drip, running cistern valve | p1 / 1.00 |
| night_flows | many small flows in one night: cistern or float valve | more than 10 flows 00-06 |

How the thresholds were set:

- One margin for all four per-hour maxima: the smallest (in steps of 0.05) whose alarms stay within about 0.75 a month on the **validation** year, with maxima taken from the training data only. It came out at **x1.35**. On the device the maxima come from all data since 2024, so the limits there are, if anything, a little quieter.
- The slow-flow indicator and the night count share the rest of the budget (about one alarm a month in total).
- Floors of 3 min and 20 L for one flow: a year of nights holds only ~120 flows, too few for a finer limit.
- On the device the duration limit is also capped at 3/4 of the Tier 1 shut-off limit (15 min at the default 20 min), so a notification always comes before the valve closes.
- What the data allows: since 2024 no normal flow ran longer than 14.2 min at any hour, and evening baths (10-14 min, ~165 L) are normal. At about one false alarm a month a daytime limit therefore cannot be much tighter than 15-20 min; the learned limits earn their keep at night and in the quiet hours.

**Honesty note.** Several detector choices were revised after seeing results, so its test numbers are somewhat optimistic: the floors and the 5 min minimum for the slow-flow indicator (after test runs), and the switch from boosted quantiles with one margin per band to per-hour maxima (after the owner saw a 39 min / 1277 L limit for Monday 19:00: one margin for the whole day had to cover the worst hour). The hourly usage model was selected on validation only.

### Synthetic leaks on the test period

There are no labelled leaks, so leaks were injected into the test year (8.4 months) at random times and merged with real flows as the firmware would (a pause under 5 s joins them). 200 per cell; recall, and the median minutes from the leak's start to the first alarm:

| Rate | 5 min | 15 min | 30 min | 60 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 L/min | 34 % (3.0) | 66 % (4.9) | 100 % (9.6) | 100 % (9.6) |
| 3 L/min | 32 % (3.0) | 72 % (7.9) | 100 % (9.6) | 100 % (9.6) |
| 6 L/min | 39 % (3.0) | 66 % (4.9) | 100 % (9.6) | 100 % (10.3) |
| 12 L/min | 33 % (3.0) | 72 % (3.9) | 100 % (9.6) | 100 % (9.6) |
| 35 L/min | 73 % (0.5) | 100 % (0.5) | 100 % (0.5) | 100 % (0.5) |

- 6 L/min for 15 min at Tuesday 03:00: 100 % after 3.0 min (median, n = 37).
- cistern refilling 0.5-2 L every 5-20 min, 00-06: 98 % after 128.0 min (median, n = 200).
- drip 0.05-0.3 L/min for 3-6 h: 100 % after 5.0 min (median, n = 200).

Reading it:

- Short flows at shower rates (3-12 L/min for 5 min) are indistinguishable from a shower and mostly pass; the device's Tier 1 limit is what covers a leak that simply keeps going.
- 15 min at tap rates is caught in 66-72 % of cases (always at night, less often in the evening, where baths are that long); 30 min and more in 100 %, after about 10 min (median).
- 35 L/min is above anything the house has drawn (peak 26.4 L/min), so it alarms in 30 s on rate alone.

False alarms per month on test, by indicator: duration 0.48, volume 0.59, rate_high 0.00, rate_low 0.00, hour_liters 0.48, night_flows 0.36.

### Compared: one multivariate score

A robust Gaussian (Minimum Covariance Determinant) of log duration and log volume per hour and day type, at the same false-alarm budget, detects far less:

| Validation leak | Mahalanobis | Per-indicator thresholds |
|---|---|---|
| leak 1 L/min x 15 min | 6 % | 100 % |
| leak 6 L/min x 15 min | 0 % | 100 % |
| leak 6 L/min x 60 min | 4 % | 100 % |
| leak 12 L/min x 30 min | 2 % | 100 % |
| tuesday 03:00 6 L/min x 15 min | 0 % | 100 % |
| cistern 0.5-2 L every 5-20 min, 00-06 | 0 % | 0 % |
| drip 0.05-0.3 L/min for 3-6 h | 20 % | 100 % |

A long flow at 12 L/min sits on the same duration-volume line as a normal one; the joint score only sees how far it is from the centre, and in two dimensions the budget buys a very wide ellipse. (The per-indicator column is flow indicators only, which is why the cistern shows 0 there; `night_flows` catches it.)

## Limits

- Almost all data comes from the legacy firmware; firmware 3.x adds two weeks. The per-flow rate and volume models inherit the legacy wrap uncertainty, and `max_lpm` / `flow_sample` (3.x only) are not used yet.
- No weather data: hot days probably explain part of the summer peaks.
- Legacy volumes are reconciled to the meter per billing interval: totals are right, but a lost flow stays missing as an event (its water is spread over the flows that were counted).
- Thresholds were set on 2025; re-running the pipeline every few months (with new meter readings) keeps them current.
